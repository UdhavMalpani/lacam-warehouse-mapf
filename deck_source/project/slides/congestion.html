Where robots wait depends on the layout

Wait actions per cell, one LaCAM* solution per layout (log scale). 1-wide lanes: 90–94 % of waits happen inside the lanes. Dead-end aisles: 83 % happen at junction cells, where the aisles open onto the corridors.

These maps count, for one LaCAM* solution per layout, how often an agent waited in each cell before reaching its goal. Where the waiting happens depends on the layout. In the MovingAI warehouse with 1-wide aisles, 90% of the waits happen inside the aisles and corridors (cells with at most two free neighbours), even though junction cells are 57% of the floor, densest in the middle of the shelving block. On the sortation floor it is 94% inside the 1-wide lanes, with a hot-spot in the centre where most routes cross. On the dead-end aisle layout the pattern flips: 83% of the waits are at junction cells, where the aisles open onto the cross corridors and side corridors, because an agent cannot enter an aisle until the agent inside has backed out. On the open cross-dock waiting is spread out and much lower, 1,907 waits for 800 agents. For layout design, this points at where wider lanes, passing bays or one-way rules would help.
